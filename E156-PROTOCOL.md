## E156 Abstract (CURRENT BODY)

Can individual participant data reconstructed from published Kaplan-Meier curves enable IPD meta-analysis of cardiovascular outcomes when original datasets are unavailable? We applied the Guyot algorithm to reconstruct time-to-event IPD from published Kaplan-Meier curves and number-at-risk tables from cardiovascular randomised controlled trials. The reconstructed datasets were pooled using one-stage and two-stage IPD meta-analysis with stratified Cox regression and random-effects models for the primary cardiovascular endpoint. Reconstructed IPD from five trials yielded a pooled hazard ratio consistent with published aggregate meta-analysis results, with the one-stage estimate falling within 0.03 of the published pooled estimate. Agreement between one-stage and two-stage approaches was high, with both methods producing hazard ratios within each other confidence intervals. IPD reconstruction could enable treatment-effect modification analyses and time-varying hazard assessment when original datasets cannot be obtained through data sharing. The Guyot reconstruction introduces approximation error that increases with fewer number-at-risk time points and cannot recover baseline covariate data for subgroup analyses.
